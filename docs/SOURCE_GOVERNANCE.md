# Source Governance

## Source hierarchy and labelling

Every indexed passage must retain its source title, URL, retrieval date (to be added), version/effective date (when available), page or section, and authority class.

Keep these classes distinct:

1. **HDFC Bank public material** — public documents published by HDFC Bank. Do not infer unpublished internal procedures.
2. **Indian law and regulation** — Acts, Rules, RBI directions, FIU-IND material, and official notifications. Check amendments and effective dates.
3. **International standards** — FATF recommendations and related official publications. These are not automatically Indian law or HDFC policy.
4. **Industry guidance** — label separately and do not present as binding law.
5. **General AML typology knowledge** — educational context, never represented as HDFC policy.

## Before indexing or relying on a source

- Confirm the URL is an official or otherwise authoritative publication.
- Record publication date, effective date, last updated date, and retrieval date where available.
- Check whether the document has been superseded or amended.
- Preserve the original file and extracted text for review.
- Check PDF extraction quality, tables, footnotes, annexures, and scanned pages.
- Do not silently resolve conflicting sources. Show the conflict and refer to the relevant effective date and authority.
- Do not treat an index page as the complete text of all linked policies.

## Abstention

When the indexed sources do not support an answer, display:

> This information is not covered in the provided policy documents.

Do not invent internal escalation thresholds, RFI templates, alert workflows, reporting timelines, or customer acceptance rules.

## Privacy and use

- Use anonymised synthetic examples only.
- Never upload real customer data, account numbers, identity documents, internal case notes, confidential policies, or proprietary employer/client material to this public repository.
- This prototype is not an official HDFC Bank system and is not for operational decisions.
- A red flag is not proof of wrongdoing.
